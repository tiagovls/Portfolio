The Problem

Today, a person who needs a prosthetic hand faces a broken system: up to a year of waiting time and costs that can reach tens of thousands of euros. Worse, the process forces the user to adapt to the prosthesis — not the other way around. Custom mold-making, extensive calibration sessions, and rigid fittings make access slow, expensive, and dehumanizing.

We founded A2M to flip that paradigm. Our goal: one universal model, low-cost, mass-producible — a prosthetic hand that learns its user, not one the user must learn to live with.

Our Solution

A2M is designing a polyarticulated prosthetic hand with two key innovations:

1. Universal fixation system — a single attachment mechanism that adapts to any residual limb (stump), eliminating the need for custom molds and lengthy fitting procedures.

2. EMG + Machine Learning personalization — EMG (electromyography) sensors read the user’s muscle signals. Combined with a companion mobile app inspired by Duolingo’s learning model, the AI continuously trains on the user’s unique patterns. Daily exercises help the system improve — the prosthesis gets smarter over time, for each individual user.

The result: a bridge prosthesis — affordable, immediately deployable, and intelligent — that serves patients while they wait for a more specialized long-term solution.

My Role

As co-founder and Vice-President — Head of R&D, I oversee both the mechanical design and the strategic R&D vision of A2M. My responsibilities include:

— CAD modeling of the hand structure and finger mechanisms (SolidWorks, Fusion 360)

— Definition of technical specifications for the universal fixation system

— Coordination of the team’s work across mechanical, electronic, and software streams

— Budget planning and resource management for prototyping

— Research into EMG signal processing and ML model architecture for user personalization
