Overview

As part of the TN12 — Industrial Systems Engineering course at UTT, our group (A26) carried out a complete technical study of an industrial forklift (chariot élévateur). The objective was to model, simulate, and size the drive system of the vehicle through rigorous analytical and numerical methods.

The entire study was conducted in Python (Jupyter Notebook), combining symbolic computation, numerical integration, and graphical output to produce a fully interactive and documented engineering report — from trajectory definition to motor power specification.

Mission Definition

The first step was to define the complete operating cycle of the forklift inside a warehouse environment. The cycle consists of 16 kinematic phases, each characterised by distance, linear velocity, angular velocity, accelerations, and duration.

Phases include: straight-line travel, 180° U-turns at constant radius, fork lifting/lowering operations, loading/unloading sequences, and transitional deceleration zones. Each phase was given a trapezoidal velocity profile — with adjustable acceleration and deceleration ramps — and validated against real forklift operating constraints.

Ph 9–16
Second pick-and-place cycle + returnMirror of phases 1–8 for the second pallet — full cycle closes back at origin P₀.

Technical Approach

The analysis was structured in three main blocks:

  1. Kinematics — speed and acceleration profiles computed per phase; total cycle time summed across all 16 segments (~60 s).
  2. Dynamics — resistive forces modelled (rolling friction, slope, inertia); driving forces and torques calculated; worst-case loading scenarios identified.
  3. Motor sizing — peak and RMS power requirements derived from force-velocity curves; drive motor pre-selected based on power budget and duty cycle.

All computations were implemented in Python (NumPy, Matplotlib, SymPy) inside a Jupyter Notebook, enabling rapid parameter sweeps and visual validation of every result.
